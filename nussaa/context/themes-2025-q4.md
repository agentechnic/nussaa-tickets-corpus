# Support Themes — Q4 2025

Prepared by the support team. Covers 1 October – 31 December 2025.

## Summary

We handled 1,840 tickets in Q4, up 12% on Q3. Volume growth tracked order
growth, so the ticket rate per order was roughly flat. Nothing this quarter
points to a single systemic cause; the mix is the usual seasonal one, with
delivery timing dominating through the December peak and dragging two other
themes up with it.

The honest read is that Q4 told us little we did not already know in Q3. The
same three themes take seven tickets in ten, and the same three fixes have
been sitting in this section of the report for three quarters running.

## Themes

| Theme | Tickets | Share | Trend vs Q3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Late delivery | 612 | 33% | ▲ 4pts |
| Wrong or missing items | 388 | 21% | ▬ flat |
| Food arrived cold | 295 | 16% | ▲ 2pts |
| Payment failed or double charged | 203 | 11% | ▼ 3pts |
| Refund not received | 147 | 8% | ▬ flat |
| App crashes or will not open | 111 | 6% | ▼ 1pt |
| Driver conduct | 84 | 5% | ▬ flat |

### Late delivery — 612 tickets (33%)

Concentrated in the last three weeks of December and peaking on weekend
evenings, when restaurant kitchens rather than drivers are the constraint.
Riyadh and Jeddah account for most of the increase; Dammam is flat.

Wording ranges from the blunt — "الطلب تأخر ساعه كامله" — through to formal
escalations that quote the delivery promise back at us: "وعدتم بنصف ساعة".
Customers using the colloquial form do the same thing more cheaply: "وعدكم نص
ساعة وصار لي ساعتين انتظر". The name is a promise and people hold us to it,
which is a good problem to have and an expensive one to answer.

### Wrong or missing items — 388 tickets (21%)

Steady across the quarter with no seasonal shape at all. Roughly two-thirds
are missing items rather than a wholly incorrect order, and missing drinks
alone account for about a fifth of the theme. These resolve quickly but the
refund follow-up is where they turn into a second ticket.

### Food arrived cold — 295 tickets (16%)

Largely a consequence of the theme above it: most of these tickets also
mention a delay, and the two rise together week by week. The residue that
does not mention a delay clusters on a small number of high-volume grilled
and fried items, which is a packaging question rather than a logistics one.

### Payment failed or double charged — 203 tickets (11%)

Down 3 points, which is the one genuinely good number in this table. The
double-charge cases are almost all authorisation holds that customers read as
a second charge; the explanation works, but only after an agent gives it.

### Refund not received — 147 tickets (8%)

Low volume, worst sentiment. Median time to resolution is more than double
any other theme, and a meaningful share of the escalations we received this
quarter began life as a refund ticket that went quiet.

## Recommendations

1. Add restaurant-side capacity signals before the next seasonal peak. Late
   delivery is a kitchen problem wearing a delivery problem's clothing, and
   we cannot staff our way past it in support.
2. Review packaging for the ten highest-volume items that arrive cold when
   the order was otherwise on time.
3. Cut refund processing time. It is the lowest-volume theme in this report
   and the one that generates the angriest customers, which is exactly the
   trade we should not be making.
4. Explain authorisation holds in the app at the moment of purchase, not in a
   reply three hours later.

## Method

Tickets were read, grouped by theme, and counted. Where a ticket raised more
than one issue it was assigned to the dominant one, which is a judgement call
and the main reason the cold-food and late-delivery counts should be read
together rather than separately. Counts are exact, not sampled. Trend
comparisons use the Q3 report's themes unchanged; no theme was renamed or
merged this quarter.
